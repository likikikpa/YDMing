STYLE "Old map ink, simple and hand-drawn": the little pictures a person draws by hand on an old chart or atlas with a dip pen and a thin brown wash. Direction, in order of importance:
1. ONLY THREE TONES. Dark brown ink (about #3b2a1a) for every line, a light-to-mid warm sepia wash (about #cdb88a) for shade and for material fills, and cream (about #f3ead0) for lit areas and surfaces left blank. NO other colour at all: no green, red, blue, no pure black, no pure white, no grey. Every object is filled (never transparent inside): foliage, roofs, rock and hulls get the sepia wash, walls and snow stay cream.
2. HUMAN, NOT MECHANICAL. A loose, slightly wobbly pen line with natural thick-and-thin variation; shapes a little irregular and asymmetric, not ruler-straight and not perfectly symmetrical; naive, warm and charming, like a historian's sketch. NOT an engraving, NOT a render, NOT clip-art, NOT a game asset.
3. SIMPLE. Few large shapes and minimal detail. A house is a roof, walls, a door, one or two windows and maybe a chimney: no individual tiles, bricks, planks or leaves; material is suggested with a few short strokes only. A tree is a trunk and three to six big bumpy leaf masses. A mountain is a few big angular ridge shapes with a handful of shade strokes. A ship is a hull, mast, simple sails and three or four rigging lines. Leave quiet areas quiet.
4. SHADING. The shaded side (bottom-right, light from the top-left) is one flat patch of sepia wash plus three to six short parallel strokes. The lit side stays cream. No gradients, no glow, no rim light, no smoke or effects.
5. NO dense hatching, NO cross-hatching mesh, NO stipple, NO grain, NO fine texture, NO microdetail anywhere.
6. LINE WEIGHT about 2.5-3.5% of the object's width, never a hairline: it must read clearly at 40 px wide.
